## What can be recovered from the CSV

The UTF-8, comma-delimited export has two tables. The observed transaction header is `DATE, TYPE, FROM ACCOUNT, TO ACCOUNT / TO CATEGORY, AMOUNT, CURRENCY, AMOUNT 2, CURRENCY 2, TAGS, NOTES`. The closing header is `NAME, BALANCE, CURRENCY`. Parse quoted records before inspecting fields. Skip blank and comma-only separator records between the tables and after the last account, including the final `,` at EOF. Accept a UTF-8 BOM, LF or CRLF, quoted commas, escaped quotes, and embedded newlines. Keep Unicode text.

Recognize the observed headers directly. For unfamiliar localized headers, let the user select the two header records in preview and map their columns one-to-one to the ten and three roles above. Require a complete header mapping before import. Decode the English and Russian type tokens below independently of the headers or device locale. Group unfamiliar tokens by spelling for explicit mapping or deferral in preview. Keep the original fields and confirmed mappings with the receipt.

Parse dates as fixed Gregorian `dd.MM.yyyy` ledger days. Both transaction amounts are unsigned magnitudes matching the entire ASCII pattern `[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)?`. The type supplies the posting sign. A closing balance uses the same grammar with an optional leading ASCII minus. A leading plus, grouping, exponent, or decimal comma is a validation error. Check field count, required values and precision on every data row. A bad transaction can be deferred. An invalid closing row identifies its record and failing field and blocks the whole commit until corrected. Only an account genuinely absent from the closing table is eligible for the transaction-only opening rule.

| Type tokens | Effect of `AMOUNT / CURRENCY` on `FROM ACCOUNT` | Meaning of the second amount and destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Income`, `Доход` | Credit the named account | The destination label identifies the income category. Preserve `AMOUNT 2 / CURRENCY 2` for the category side. |
| `Expense`, `Расход` | Debit the named account | The destination label identifies the expense category. Preserve its amount and currency. |
| `Transfer`, `Перевод` | Debit the source account | Credit the destination account with `AMOUNT 2 / CURRENCY 2`. The two amounts can have different currencies. |

The sample has 5,605 transaction rows (4,397 expenses, 219 incomes, and 989 transfers), 15 account snapshots, and six currency codes across both tables. Dates run newest-first. Some converted amounts have 19 fractional digits. Tags are empty in this export. Notes include Unicode and commas. Comparing all ten fields produces 42 repeat occurrences beyond the first copy of each distinct row.

1. **Preview.** Show type counts, currencies, mappings, and row errors. Propose the common `CURRENCY 2` of income and expense rows as the reporting currency and ask the user to confirm. The sample has one such code. A mixed set needs a currency choice and conversion of any unmatched category amounts. On a transfer, that column denominates the receiving account. Match accounts by source label and currency. If that pair occurs twice or more in the closing table, require the user to map each closing row to a distinct account and assign each affected transaction leg before booking it. Seed new accounts' positions from the closing table, including snapshot-only accounts. Its first eight rows match the visible account list. Append transaction-only accounts in first-appearance order and retain existing user order on later imports. Interpret `Parent (Child)` within the income or expense kind, preserve the source label, and allow mapping corrections.
2. **Confirm the snapshot day.** The CSV supplies no export date. Suggest the latest valid transaction day in the file before skips or deferrals and ask the user to confirm the actual day represented by the closing balances. A snapshot-only file requires the user to enter that day. A source day after the proposed cutoff blocks commit and identifies the offending rows until the user corrects the day or the file. The cutoff includes the entire confirmed ledger day. Use it for reconciliation, cutoff rate lookup, and the date of each snapshot adjustment.
3. **Reconcile.** Offer to establish openings when the ledger has no receipt that owns them, even if someone has entered a manual expense. Seven of the 15 reference balances differ from replaying the entries from zero. For each mapped closing row, infer the opening from its balance minus the **original signed native postings** of the receipt's unique accepted source occurrences through the cutoff. Store one native opening per imported account before the history, including zero. For a transaction-only account, disclose the assumed zero opening. Show manual movements separately in the projected live balances. Later files reuse these openings and store a zero opening for each newly mapped account. Preview each later snapshot difference for explicit acceptance as a dated balance adjustment, using the retry calculation below.
4. **Commit.** Save financial records, search keys, mappings, order, reconciliation decisions, original contributions and row outcomes in one SQLite transaction. Every receipt retains its closing rows, confirmed cutoff, and accepted or deferred adjustment decisions. Reuse them on retry. The opening owner's receipt also records any pending combined valuation. Each confirmed reporting opening retains its own original combined closing value and valuation evidence. A failed commit rolls back the batch. A completed receipt means the imported entries are already searchable.

### Import identity and repeat imports

LedgerCore owns the import identity encoding and hashing on Linux and iOS. Use the `Crypto` product from Apple's `swift-crypto` Swift package. Use `SHA256` for file bytes, source fingerprints, and booked-payload hashes, and `Insecure.SHA1` for the RFC-defined UUIDv5 construction. The v1 namespace is `22567d33-1f45-5af1-b0a3-843ee64f7b3a`. Form the name from the type tag, lowercase hex file digest, then the key fields below. Normalize each field to NFC and prefix its UTF-8 bytes with its decimal byte count and `#`, then concatenate. Ordinals use unsigned decimal text with no leading zeros. Keep this format stable across parser upgrades. [Swift Crypto](https://github.com/apple/swift-crypto) · [UUIDv5 specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9562.html#section-5.5)

| Record created by import | Key within that file |
| --- | --- |
| `receipt` | No additional fields |
| `account` | Original account label and uppercase native currency code. If that pair repeats in the closing table, append the zero-based ordinal among closing rows with that pair. |
| `category` | Canonical kind (`Income` or `Expense`), source parent, then source child if present. An implicit parent uses the parent-only key. |
| `transaction`, `receipt-row` | Zero-based transaction record ordinal. Count nonempty data records before validation or filtering, including rows the user skips. |
| `opening`, `snapshot-adjustment` | Use the source account key above, including its ordinal for a repeated pair. |
| `reporting-opening` | Original uppercase reporting currency, confirmed cutoff total, and method (`rates` for valued balances or `total` for a supplied cutoff total). For `rates`, append one uppercase code and confirmed rate per distinct foreign closing currency, in ASCII code order. Rates are reporting units per native unit. Use the canonical decimals below. |

For the v1 category key, first check that the label's literal parentheses are balanced. If it ends in `)`, find the matching opening `(` by nesting depth. Split only if that opening follows a literal ASCII space and both resulting names are nonempty. Remove that one space and the outer pair, preserving all other characters. Thus `Travel (Hotels (EU))` has parent `Travel` and child `Hotels (EU)`. An unbalanced label, an empty suffix such as `Travel ()`, or a label without this delimiter is a parent in full.

Across file digests, look up ledger-scoped aliases by original account label and currency or by category kind and source hierarchy. Reuse an unambiguous target and attach the new file-specific ID. Every account mapping must match the target's current native currency. For a repeated account key, save the user's closing-row and posting assignments with their file ordinals. A later file requires explicit mapping for that ambiguous key. For an unseen key, preview a match to an existing target or create one. Keep source aliases through account renames and category remapping. A changed source label can be explicitly mapped to the same target. Import, backup, and sync preserve these mappings.

A receipt row records its ordinal, fingerprint, occurrence among equal rows, target when known, and outcome (applied, linked, deferred by the user, deferred with error, or intentionally skipped). For an applied occurrence, retain the initial payload hash, mapped account IDs, original day, and immutable signed native legs and currencies. Retain the signed reporting contribution and currency when confirmed (zero for an internal transfer). An applied row can still await reporting valuation. A later confirmation values the original source amounts for reconciliation, even if a live edit or deletion requires a different live contribution. Linked rows point to the same original occurrence. Record a deletion in the receipt in the same transaction as the tombstone.

Fingerprint the ten decoded **source** fields in canonical transaction-header order using SHA-256 over the same length-prefixed encoding, independent of file digest and target edits. Encode the day as Gregorian `YYYY-MM-DD`, the mapped type as exactly `Income`, `Expense`, or `Transfer`, and currency codes as uppercase ASCII. Encode decimals in plain base ten without an exponent or leading plus. Keep one integer zero for a value below one in magnitude. Remove redundant integer zeros and trailing fractional zeros, then remove the decimal point if the fraction is empty. All forms of zero become `0`, and `3.50` and `3.5` both become `3.5`. Account and category labels, tags, and notes use NFC **untrimmed**, preserving case, leading and trailing spaces, and embedded newlines. CSV quoting and record terminators are framing. For a validation error, retain a separately tagged hash of the decoded fields and the source values needed to retry.

- **Retry the same file.** Retry either kind of deferral, or reopen an intentional skip on request. For the opening owner, recompute each snapshot-derived native opening from its closing row and each reporting opening from its own confirmed cutoff value. Subtract the corresponding original contributions of that receipt's unique accepted occurrences through the cutoff, including newly accepted rows and the original amounts of edited or deleted occurrences.
- **Recompute later snapshots.** On initial reconciliation and every retry, process later snapshots for each affected account in ascending order of cutoff and receipt UUID. Show competing closing values for the same day in preview. The balance before an adjustment is the native opening plus the original signed legs of all unique accepted import occurrences through that cutoff, plus preceding accepted snapshot adjustments. The required adjustment is the stored closing balance minus this balance. Exclude the adjustment being solved for, all following adjustments, and local manual movements. A linked occurrence counts once. Recalculate downstream adjustments when an earlier input changes.
- **Confirm changes together.** Preview the revised openings and adjustments beside live balances, which include local edits, deletions and manual entries. On confirmation, update the derived records under their original IDs, including an accepted adjustment revised to zero. Value a revised foreign adjustment at its stored confirmed cutoff rate, obtain a revised reporting amount, or mark its valuation pending. Commit the new rows, valuations, reconciliation decisions and revisions together. Record manual corrections as separate entries. Unaccepted differences still require a decision. A retry with identical inputs and decisions writes no revisions.
- **Overlapping file.** Match fingerprint occurrences one-to-one against the original import records, including those linked to edited or deleted entries. For ambiguous account labels, confirm the original posting assignments before linking. Count each original occurrence once even if several receipts refer to it. Link a match to its original target, or carry forward its deferral or intentional skip. Preview excess occurrences and ambiguous changes. Flag a missing occurrence only if its original source day falls between the earliest and latest valid transaction days in the new file, inclusive, before skips and deferrals. A file with no dated transactions yields no missing-row candidates. Keep missing rows by default. Removing a row or restoring a deletion requires an explicit decision.
- **Delete data.** Before resetting a paired ledger, check for unacknowledged changes, including in-flight or conflicted groups. Show the count and offer Sync first, Cancel, or explicitly discard the unsent changes on this device. Sync first waits for durable acknowledgements and rechecks the queue. An offline or failed sync leaves the ledger available for retry. Serialize the final check and reset with ledger writes. A confirmed reset detaches sync, clears the local ledger including drafts, receipts, aliases, indexes, outbox, and cursors, then starts a fresh unpaired ledger. Remote data and separately exported files keep their own lifetime.

Unused categories, category order, account group assignments, appearance, archive flags, preferences, and historical exchange rates need another source. Use editable defaults. Include import receipts and mappings in the versioned 6Money backup so restoring a backup preserves re-import behavior.

The iPhone references show a CSV export command in Settings and a period picker in Categories. The CSV carries no declared export range, and the Android reference reads 1Money exports. Whether the legacy iPhone exporter itself offers a date-range selector is unverified.

## App and package layout

Use native SwiftUI for iPhone and a SwiftPM package rooted at `Package.swift` for the portable code. Keep everything except drawing and iOS services in the package so it builds and runs on Kubuntu. Plan for iOS 17 as the initial deployment target and confirm it on the intended phone when the first TestFlight build is installed. The proposed paths below are implementation milestones.

| Location | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `Sources/LedgerCore/` | Value types, entry commands, account and category rules, period boundaries, report calculations, import identity, and the store interface. Uses portable Foundation APIs and Swift Crypto. |
| `Sources/LedgerStore/`, `Sources/CSQLite/` | SQLite implementation and a SwiftPM system-library target linking the platform's SQLite. Own schema versions, transactional migrations, foreign keys, writes, and queries. |
| `Sources/OneMoneyCSV/` | Decode the two tables and produce typed import drafts using LedgerCore. The store commits an accepted draft through the same write path used by manual entry. |
| `Sources/LedgerPresentation/` | Screen state and display models for the four tabs, Settings, and the entry, account, and period sheets. Pure functions turn committed ledger state and view state into rows, section headers, period cards, ring segments, formatted amounts, and accessibility label text, and validate an entry form before it submits a command. A plain-text renderer of each display model serves the snapshot tests and the command-line tool. Portable, with no SwiftUI. |
| `Sources/SixMoneyCLI/` | The `sixmoney` executable for hands-on use on Kubuntu. It opens a ledger file, previews and commits an import, records and edits entries, and prints any screen through the text renderer. It uses the same commands, store, and display models as the app. |
| `ios/project.yml`, `ios/SixMoney/` | The tracked XcodeGen spec defines the app, UI tests, local package, and shared `SixMoney` scheme. Use `syncedFolder` sources with project format `xcode16_0` or later. The Mac session scripts generate `ios/SixMoney.xcodeproj` on macOS. The app draws the display models in SwiftUI and owns Files import/export, iOS lifecycle, file protection, and Keychain. [XcodeGen project specification](https://github.com/yonaskolb/XcodeGen/blob/master/Docs/ProjectSpec.md) |
| `Tests/` | SwiftPM tests with invented ledgers, CSV fixtures, temporary databases, and tracked text snapshots of each screen's display model. Simulator UI tests live with the iOS project. |

On Save, the view submits one command. The store commits it and publishes the change. The account list, daily history, ring, and Overview query the same committed state. The tab, filter, period selection, and typing before Save are view state, held as LedgerPresentation values so their transitions run in Linux tests. Save pending retains an unfinished manual draft across launches. Posting a complete native movement can leave its reporting valuation pending.

Format amounts in LedgerPresentation from the exact decimal and the ledger's number-format setting. Snapshot tests pin that setting, the locale, the calendar, and the time zone, so a snapshot shows the text a screen displays on either platform.

## Persistent state and accounting rules

| Record | Fields and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Ledger and preferences | A stable ledger UUID, reporting currency, receipt that owns the openings, active reporting-opening ID once valued, and week/month boundaries. Theme, startup tab, and formatting are device-local. Store device-lock credentials in Keychain. |
| Account | UUID, name, native currency, icon, color, sort position, star, and archive state. Currency is editable until the first posting locks it permanently. An import commits the lock with the native opening, including a zero opening for a snapshot-only account. To switch currencies, open an account in the new currency, transfer the balance at the agreed amounts, and optionally archive the old account. |
| Category | UUID, income/expense kind, optional parent ID, name, icon, color, sort position, and archive state. Support adding, renaming, reordering, and archiving parents and children. Equal names can occur under different parents or kinds. |
| Entry | UUID, ledger day, per-day sequence, kind, signed native postings, note, tags, and optional provenance. Income and expense require a category and retain the original category-side amount and currency. A **balance adjustment** has one signed native leg, an origin (manual correction, outside funding, or receipt snapshot), and no category. For each non-transfer posting, full booking requires a confirmed signed amount in the ledger's reporting currency. A transfer commits both native legs atomically and contributes zero to all-accounts flows. Edits and reversals are atomic. |
| Manual draft | Device-local, included in local backups. Retain its UUID, selections, known amounts and missing input. Posting promotes that ID once and assigns its sequence and native postings in the same transaction. A draft has no postings. |
| Valuation rate | Source currency, target currency, effective ledger day, and exact positive target units per source unit. Keep one active confirmed rate per pair and day, with superseded quotes as evidence. Selecting a rates-based cutoff valuation selects its quotes for that day. |
| Change metadata | Ledger-scoped record IDs, local revision and change sequence, deletion markers, and a schema version. Import receipts and their mappings survive restarts. |

Represent a money value as a signed base-10 coefficient and scale plus currency. Support at least 38 coefficient digits at every scale from 0 through 19 for amounts, balances, and rates. Store the coefficient as digit text with its sign in SQLite and JSON. Use exact sums and widened intermediate products, including when multiplying two scale-19 inputs. Round only for display or an explicitly confirmed conversion. Report unsupported precision or overflow in preview before commit. SQLite numeric aggregation on decimal text can coerce it to floating point, so sum through the core's exact arithmetic path. [SQLite aggregate functions](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_aggfunc.html)

The Balance action posts the difference between the requested native balance and the account balance on the chosen ledger day. Value each income, expense and balance adjustment on its ledger day. Copy a source amount already in the reporting currency, preferring the recorded category side on import. Otherwise propose the latest confirmed rate for the pair effective on or before that day, or accept a user-supplied reporting amount. Apply the native posting's direction and record the confirmed amount, currency and valuation evidence. For a snapshot adjustment use the receipt's cutoff day. Subsequent rate changes affect proposals and holdings valuations. Historical flows use the amount booked for each posting.

If only the reporting amount is missing, the user can **post the native amount** and complete its valuation later. The store commits the signed native leg, sequence, and pending valuation together. The entry immediately feeds native balances and native reports, survives restart, and travels in backup and sync. An affected flow-based total shows the entries awaiting valuation until all required amounts are confirmed. A CSV export that requires a missing amount identifies the entries to finish before writing. Confirming the amount updates the same entry and revision history in one transaction, without adding another native leg. Saving a manual draft or deferring an entire import row leaves that movement unposted.

A populated ledger's reporting-currency change must also restate each retained reporting opening, its cutoff value, and the original contributions used for retries in the new basis. Preview this together with the conversion of live reporting movements, resolve missing amounts before switching, and preserve the original booking and valuation evidence.

### Period balances

The Transactions period cards use recorded flows. Establish the combined closing value at the **CSV snapshot cutoff**. Prefer valuing the exported native closing balances with confirmed cutoff rates. A total copied from the old app must describe those same balances, including any changes on the closing day. If the only total available is later, roll it back only if every intervening movement and valuation change is known. Show the adjustment and require confirmation. When both valuations are available, surface any discrepancy before applying an opening. The user can defer the reporting opening while using native balances and confirmed flows.

For the opening owner, each reporting opening is its **confirmed cutoff value minus the original reporting contributions** of its unique accepted source occurrences through the cutoff. If one of those contributions awaits confirmation, retain the valuation decision and mark its opening pending until it can be recomputed. The original valuation inputs identify that decision across retries and currency restatements. The combined period cards add the live booked reporting movements through the selected day to the single active reporting opening. A missing opening or a pending contribution in that cumulative balance marks the card pending. Period flows need only the contributions within that period. Native openings seed the individual accounts. A new ledger starts at zero. Later outside funding is a balance adjustment. Funding from a tracked account is a transfer.

The **Accounts header and section subtotal** show the value of the native balances listed in their respective scopes. For each nonzero foreign balance, multiply by the latest confirmed reporting-units-per-native-unit rate effective on or before the displayed day. The reporting currency contributes its native amount. Any zero balance contributes zero even without a rate. Sum exactly, then round for display. Show the valuation day and rates used. If a required rate is missing, show native currency totals and identify the missing rate in place of the combined figure. A supplied cutoff total establishes a reporting opening only. Label the Accounts total as holdings value and the Transactions cards as recorded balances.

Use the same account filter and period for Transactions, the category ring, and Overview. Start immediately before the period's first day and end after its last day.

| Scope | Ring and Overview income and spending | Daily net, period cards, and Overview net change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All accounts | Categorized income and expenses in booked reporting amounts | Signed reporting flows including confirmed balance adjustments. Internal transfers net to zero, including across currencies. Show adjustments separately from spending and income. |
| One account | Categorized movements touching that account in its native currency, using its native amounts | Opening plus native postings gives the balance. Sum the day's or period's native postings for the change, including incoming and outgoing transfer legs and corrections. Show net transfers and adjustments separately from categorized totals. |

For fully valued period cards, the ending balance equals the starting balance plus net movement at stored precision. Identify any minor-unit remainder caused by rounding the display totals.

The supplied Transactions screenshot is one expense later than the CSV snapshot. Subtracting that expense from the CSV's annual signed category flows reproduces the change between the screenshot's cards to the cent. The affected account's native balance also fell by that expense. A closing total copied directly from this later screen would shift the inferred reporting opening. Use the export cutoff when reconciling these references.

### Ordering, periods, and search

- History sorts by day, per-day sequence, then stable entry UUID, all descending. Include reserved slots for deferred or skipped rows with a known day. If the incoming day has no matched rows, allocate its `n` rows as one block above all existing entries and reservations. Use base 0 on an empty day, otherwise one more than the day's maximum sequence. The newest-first source row at position `i` gets `base + n - 1 - i`. When matches supply neighbors, insert unmatched runs in source order beside those anchors, resequencing the day's entries and reservations together if necessary. Preserve the relative order of existing entries. A retry fills the original reservation. A manual insert or an edit moving to another day takes the next free sequence above that day's maximum. Allocate positions in the write transaction. Same-day edits retain their position. Stable UUIDs break ties from concurrent offline inserts.
- For a month starting on day 29, 30, or 31, clamp independently to each month's last day. A period includes its start and excludes the next start. With day 31 selected, February starts on the 28th or 29th and March starts on the 31st. Apply these boundaries and the configured week start consistently to all period views.
- LedgerCore builds a locale-independent Unicode case-folded, NFC-normalized key for each searchable label, note, and tag and for the query. Match literal substrings with SQLite `instr(key, ?)`. Store keys with their owning records in the same write transaction on import, edit, and rename. Search joins current label keys by ID. A folding-version migration rebuilds keys before the ledger opens. Keep original strings for display and fingerprinting. New-entry pickers omit archived items. [SQLite expression behavior](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_expr.html)

## Adding automatic sync later

On first launch, create a local ledger with a device-generated UUID. The ledger works offline and needs no login. Commit each mutation and its change metadata together, including imports and deletions. A future sync worker can read this store and use an injected transport to push changes and pull updates after a server cursor.

1. **Pair a ledger.** Require authenticated, ledger-scoped access before seeding a remote ledger, downloading its snapshot and cursor onto an empty device, or explicitly merging independently populated ledgers. On opt-in, capture a consistent snapshot at local change sequence S and queue later writes at that boundary. When merging, match portable source IDs, then use source aliases and the overlapping-file review for different digests. Sync receipts, original contributions, per-row outcomes, aliases, and tombstones with their targets. Carry forward one-sided edits or deletions and resolve divergent changes.

   For a shared receipt, resolve any competing cutoff days or mappings, merge accepted occurrences once, and recompute openings and later snapshot adjustments from the original contributions. Merge confirmations with the same valuation key into one reporting opening and preserve their evidence. For different owners, reconcile one native opening per mapped account. Preview competing reporting openings against the merged movements. The user selects one or confirms a reconciled replacement. Retain the alternatives and their evidence as inactive provenance and commit the selected owner and single active reporting-opening ID atomically.

2. **Send changes.** During pairing from sequence S and thereafter, each local write to synced records queues its outbox group in the same transaction. Unpaired writes retain local change metadata only. Give each group an immutable operation ID, payload schema version, and immutable payload. For each affected record, store its known remote base revision or a dependency on the last pending operation for that record. Wait for predecessors and use their acknowledged revisions. Persist the resolved request and retry it verbatim after a timeout. The server checks all expected revisions and atomically applies the group and its idempotency record. On acknowledgement, persist the returned revisions and predecessor results needed by dependents and remove the acknowledged group in the same local transaction. A conflict pauses dependents. An intervening remote edit requires conflict resolution if it invalidates the expected revision. A large import can send several groups in dependency order. Both transfer postings travel in one group.
3. **Receive and resolve.** Apply incoming changes, including tombstones, together with the next cursor in one local transaction. Mark their origin as remote so the outbox queues only local edits. Use remote revisions to detect concurrent edits and edit/delete conflicts. Preserve both versions of a conflicting financial entry for user resolution as one new revision. Keep tombstones until peers have acknowledged them or require a fresh snapshot for a stale cursor.
4. **Schedule work.** Sync on foreground entry, after a save, and when connectivity returns. Persist progress and retry transient failures with backoff. Use iOS background refresh as an additional opportunity subject to system scheduling. Surface pending changes, last successful sync, and conflicts. Keep credentials in Keychain. [Apple background task guidance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/backgroundtasks/choosing-background-strategies-for-your-app)

Choose the remote service when the sharing requirements are known. A private CloudKit database is a candidate for one person's Apple devices. If household or cross-platform sharing is needed, evaluate a service with authenticated per-ledger access. [CloudKit private database](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/ckcontainer/privateclouddatabase)

The Android reference combines PowerSync and Supabase. Its `OneMoneyConvert.kt`, `DbSchema.kt`, and `AtomicCrudSupabaseConnector.kt` illustrate two-sided entries and grouped writes. The Russian type tokens in the CSV contract come from the converter. The observed headers and English tokens come from the local iPhone export. Write the importer independently.

## Acceptance examples

### Milestone 2 · Local import and reports

The fictional fixture has four transactions. It includes duplicate expenses with a quoted Cyrillic note containing a comma, a same-day transfer, two separators between tables, and a comma-only record after the final account. Its checked-in LF bytes give receipt UUID `4206b8be-10f9-52a5-bf91-c435f3eabca2`. Confirm the suggested cutoff of March 2, 2024 and reporting currency USD. Seed Pocket EUR before Envelope USD and store their two native openings, 4 EUR and 0 USD. At the closing rate of 1.25 USD per EUR, the 6 EUR and 10 USD held are worth 17.50 USD. Each 3 EUR expense booked 4.50 USD. Recorded flows total +11.00 USD, so the reporting opening is **6.50 USD**. Valuing the native openings at the closing rate gives 5.00 USD. [Legacy CSV fixture](../Tests/OneMoneyCSVTests/Fixtures/legacy-ledger.csv)

#### Balances and reporting

- On March 1, income takes the combined balance from 6.50 to 26.50 USD. On March 2, the all-accounts ring shows 9.00 USD spent. The day net is −9.00 USD and the combined cards close at 17.50 USD. Filter to Pocket EUR and the ring and Overview show 6 EUR spent. The incoming transfer of 8 EUR yields a daily net of +2 EUR and native cards from 4 to 6 EUR. History shows the first expense, the second expense, then the transfer. Searching for `проба` finds both expenses.
- Use Balance to correct Pocket EUR from 6 to 10 EUR. Confirm the +4 EUR adjustment at 1.25 USD per EUR. Its +5.00 USD reporting posting takes the recorded closing card to 22.50 USD. The Accounts header and subtotal also show 22.50 USD. The adjustment appears separately from category spending and income.
- Starting again at the fixture cutoff, spend the remaining 6 EUR booked at 6.00 USD and the remaining 10 USD. Both native balances are zero. The Accounts header and section subtotal each show **0.00 USD**. Transactions shows a recorded closing balance of **1.50 USD** (17.50 − 6.00 − 10.00).
